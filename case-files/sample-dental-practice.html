Case file: dental practice

Industry: dental practice · Audit date: sample data · Findings: 5 (2 critical, 2 warnings, 1 pass)

What we found

Appointment reminders and treatment-plan emails were landing in spam for Gmail patients. The audit found why in the first ten minutes of checks:

CRITICAL

No DMARC record: anyone can spoof this domain

No DMARC policy published at all. Forged mail claiming to be the practice, appointment scams, fake invoices, is delivered by receiving servers with no questions asked.

TXT  _dmarc   "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@[domain]"
CRITICAL

SPF record authorizes the entire internet

An SPF record exists, but it ends in +all, which tells receiving servers that literally any server on earth may send mail for this domain. It is worse than no SPF at all, because it actively vouches for forgeries.

TXT  @   "v=spf1 mx ~all"  (replace the +all with ~all)
WARNING

No DKIM key under common selectors

No DKIM public key was found under the standard provider selectors we probe. Outbound mail is likely going out unsigned, so receivers cannot cryptographically verify it came from the practice.

WARNING

One mail server missing reverse DNS

One of the two MX hosts has no PTR record. Some receiving servers penalize or defer mail from hosts without reverse DNS, which adds delivery delays.

PASS

Not on major blocklists

Both mail server IPs came back clean on the blocklists checked. The problem is authentication, not reputation, which is the good kind of problem to have.

What was fixed

  • Published a DMARC record starting in monitoring mode (p=none with a reporting address), with a written plan to move to p=quarantine after two weeks of clean reports.
  • Rewrote the SPF record to remove the +all and end in ~all, keeping the practice's actual senders (office mail and the appointment-reminder service).
  • Gave the practice the exact DNS entries to ask their mail provider for DKIM signing, and a note for their IT person on adding the PTR record.
