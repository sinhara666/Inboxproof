What a report looks like

Real examples of findings from real audits:

CRITICAL

No DMARC record: anyone can spoof your domain

Without DMARC, receiving servers have no instruction for mail that fails authentication, so forged mail from your domain is delivered normally.

TXT  _dmarc   "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com"
CRITICAL

No SPF record: your mail fails a basic authenticity check

Receiving servers check SPF to confirm the sending server is allowed to send for your domain. With no record, that check cannot pass.

TXT  @   "v=spf1 mx ~all"
WARNING

DMARC is in monitoring mode only (p=none)

Your DMARC record collects reports but tells receivers to deliver failing mail anyway. Spoofed mail still reaches inboxes.

PASS

DKIM key published

A DKIM public key is published and resolvable, so receivers can verify your signatures.

FAQ

Do I need a domain? What about my @gmail.com address?
The audit checks a domain's DNS records, so you need your own domain to audit. Plain @gmail.com, @yahoo.com, @outlook.com addresses can't be audited, there are no records of yours to check. If you send from your own domain (even a personal one), you're good.
Do you implement the fixes?
No. The report gives you the exact DNS records to add and where they go, written so you or your DNS provider can apply them in minutes. Implementation isn't offered, the report is the product.
How is this different from a free checker?
Free tools dump raw data at you. This is a reviewed report: findings ranked by impact, written in plain English, each with the exact fix record. You don't need to know what DMARC is to use it.
What do you need from me?
Just your domain name and an email to send the report to. No passwords, no DNS access, no logins. Everything is checked from public DNS records.
What if my setup is already perfect?
Then the report says so, with the evidence. A clean bill of health with proof is worth knowing too.
Is my data shared or sold?
No. Your domain and report are used only to deliver your audit. Nothing is sold, shared, or published.
